\section{Use Case}

Students, researchers and other stakeholders who need information about universities worldwide
face a fragmented landscape: registries, knowledge bases and quality assurance databases differ
in format, definitions and coverage, which makes institutions hard to compare. The Research
Organization Registry (ROR) has reliable names and locations, but few descriptive facts. Wikidata
adds founding years, student numbers, institution types and memberships, but it is incomplete.
The Database of External Quality Assurance Results (DEQAR) of the European Quality Assurance
Register for Higher Education (EQAR) records the official name of an institution, its mergers
and closures and its external quality assurance reports, but 89\% of its institutions lie in the
European Higher Education Area.

We integrate these three sources into one coherent dataset that describes every university
exactly once. Students can look up where a university is located, how old and how large it is
and whether it has been quality-assured; analysts, policy makers and university administrators
can compare institutions without consolidating sources by hand.

\paragraph{What we count as a university.}
A university in this project is a higher education institution that awards academic degrees and
is an organisation of its own. A college or an institute that meets both conditions counts as a
university, whatever it is called; a branch, a faculty or a campus does not. The sources draw
this line differently, so two kinds of records need care. (i)~\emph{Parts of a university}: the
type \emph{education} of ROR also holds branches; 2,786 of its records name a parent
organisation, e.g.\ ``Islamic Azad University South Tehran Branch''. DEQAR lists faculties,
schools and departments
of Spanish universities (1,275 records with a parent institution, such as ``Faculty of Law'')
and single sites of French schools.
(ii)~\emph{Predecessors}: DEQAR keeps 916 closed institutions and records mergers, absorptions
and split-offs for 1,054 institutions, e.g.\ Paris-Sorbonne University, closed in 2018 and
succeeded by Sorbonne Universit\'e. A part is never merged with the university it belongs to,
and a predecessor is a different entity than its successor, whereas a former name of the same
institution is one of its alternative names. Such records stay in the input data; they are the
hard non-matches of our gold standard.

\section{Datasets: Schema and Basic Profile}

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} profiles the three datasets. Each contains a single class,
\emph{University}, so the number of records per class is the number of entities in the table.

\paragraph{Data sources and preprocessing.}
Our Python scripts retrieved Wikidata and ROR on 2 October 2026 and DEQAR on 9 October 2026 and
store each source as one CSV file with one row per university. Attributes with several values
are kept as lists, and website links that differ only in \texttt{http}/\texttt{https} or
\texttt{www} are merged.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Wikidata}, \url{https://query.wikidata.org/sparql}: all items that are an instance
of \emph{university} (Q3918) or of one of its subclasses. Identifiers of countries, places and
types were replaced by English labels; per university we kept the earliest founding year, the
preferred or most recent student number and the city of its headquarters or location.
\item \emph{ROR}, data dump v2.13 of 22 September 2026,
\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22902037}: of its 141,528 records we kept the 30,131 of
type \emph{education} that are not withdrawn.
\item \emph{DEQAR}, \url{https://www.eqar.eu/qa-results/download-data-sets/}: the list of higher
education institutions that EQAR publishes as a CSV file and refreshes every night. It is based
on records of the European Tertiary Education Register (ETER) and on entries by the quality
assurance agencies registered with EQAR. We merged the rows that repeat an institution once per
location, kept the year of the founding and closure dates and, where the file gives no country,
took the country from the code that starts the ETER identifier.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Missing values.}
Over all attributes, 32\% of the values are missing in Wikidata, 17\% in ROR and 39\% in DEQAR.
In DEQAR this is partly by nature: a closure year and a history exist only for institutions
that were closed or merged. The attributes we match on are well filled: the name is never
missing, and the country is missing in 3\% of the Wikidata, 1\% of the DEQAR and none of the ROR
records. The sources fill each other's gaps: the founding year is missing in 19\% of the
Wikidata and 41\% of the DEQAR records but in only 11\% of the ROR records, and the coordinates
missing in 31\% of the Wikidata records are complete in ROR.

\section{Integrated Schema and Overlap with Input Schemata}

The integrated schema has one class, \emph{University}, with 21 attributes
(Table~\ref{tab:schema}): 11 are contained in at least two datasets, five other than the name
in all three (for fusion by voting), and seven are lists. No single attribute serves as a key:
the source identifiers are not part of the schema, and the name alone is ambiguous (in ROR,
``National University'' exists in four countries). A university is identified by the
combination of name and country, unique for 99.1\% of the Wikidata, 99.8\% of the ROR and
94.5\% of the DEQAR records; in DEQAR the Spanish faculties share names such as ``Faculty of
Law'', and with the city and the parent institution 99.8\% of its records are unique.

The mapping is not one-to-one everywhere: ROR names a parent organisation by its identifier,
which we replace by the name, and the \emph{types} of ROR only hold registry categories such as
\emph{education} or \emph{funder}, so they are not mapped to the institution types.

\section{Entities Contained in Multiple Datasets}

\paragraph{Estimate based on small samples.}
We drew two random samples of 100 records. Of 100 Wikidata universities, 56 carry a link to a
ROR record, which extrapolates to about 12,700 entities in at least two datasets. Of 100 DEQAR
institutions, 41 carry a link to a ROR record; we checked the other 59 by hand and found 19 more
in ROR or Wikidata, so 60 are contained in another dataset, which extrapolates to about 5,400.
Both estimates are far above the required 1,000.

\paragraph{Check on the complete files.}
Wikidata and ROR reference each other through ROR and Wikidata identifiers, and DEQAR carries
ROR identifiers. Following these links groups the 61,807 records into 45,081 distinct
institutions, 14,372 of them in at least two sources and 2,183 in all three
(Table~\ref{tab:requirements}); an exact comparison of names and countries raises these lower
bounds to 14,658 and 2,627. The identifiers are removed from the matching input, and names alone
do not suffice: 25\% of the linked Wikidata--ROR and 52\% of the linked DEQAR--ROR pairs differ
in name even after normalisation.

\paragraph{Gold standard.}
The links give us matching pairs, but they are incomplete: 19 of the 60 DEQAR sample
institutions that are contained in another dataset carry no link. We therefore do not derive
non-matches from missing links; we check pairs that look alike but are not linked by hand and
label them as match or non-match. We add corner cases by hand: a faculty, site or branch against
its university (16 of the 59 unlinked sample records are such parts), a closed predecessor
against its successor (2 of the 59), and universities that share a name in different countries.

\paragraph{Distinct sources.}
Compared with our first abstract we dropped THE and DBpedia. DBpedia is extracted from the
English Wikipedia, on which Wikidata partly draws as well: 63\% of the linked Wikidata--DBpedia
pairs have coordinates that are identical to about ten metres. The remaining sources have
different origins: Wikidata is edited by volunteers, ROR is curated by its own team, and DEQAR
is based on ETER and on the entries of quality assurance agencies. On linked records the
founding year of DEQAR equals that of ROR in only 73\% and that of Wikidata in 74\% of the cases.
DEQAR is the smallest dataset, but about 60\% of its institutions are contained in another
dataset, and it shares five attributes besides the name with both.
